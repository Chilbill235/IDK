About SafeBeef

A browser-based pentest simulator built to teach security concepts through safe, hands-on experience. No real systems are harmed.

v83 · Educational use only · © 2025
ℹ
Educational Purpose
SafeBeef is a controlled learning environment. Every "attack" you see is simulated locally between your own browser tabs. Nothing leaves your device, nothing targets a real person, and nothing persists across sessions unless you explicitly opt in.

What SafeBeef teaches

Modern web apps are complex, and the most effective way to understand their vulnerabilities is to see them exploited safely. SafeBeef replicates the visual style and interaction model of real penetration-testing toolkits — the BeEF hook, iMessage-style chat, XSS payloads, session hooks — while keeping everything sandboxed.

🎣

Hook Engine

Simulates how a compromised browser "hooks" into a target and reports back activity — without touching any real network.

🧩

50+ Modules

Each module demonstrates a real-world class of attack: XSS, clickjacking, fingerprinting, social engineering, and more.

💬

iMessage UI

A faithful reproduction of Apple's Messages app so social-engineering lessons feel realistic, not contrived.

🛡

Moderation Tools

Privileged accounts can warn, mute, freeze, and ban users — with full audit logs and appeal handling.

🔐

Role System

Owner, moderator, and user roles with fine-grained permissions enforced on every sensitive action.

📡

Live Everything

BroadcastChannel, storage events, and a reactive hub keep every tab in sync — no page reloads required.

What SafeBeef will never do

  • Transmit data. Every demo is local. Your credentials, messages, and files never leave your browser.
  • Execute real exploits. Modules simulate behavior — they don't call real APIs or attack real infrastructure.
  • Track you. No analytics, no third-party scripts, no telemetry.
  • Show you someone else's data. Only your own browser tabs appear as "targets."

Who it's for

SafeBeef is designed for:

  • Students learning web security fundamentals
  • Instructors building interactive workshop content
  • Developers who want to see what "the other side" of a pentest looks like
  • Anyone curious about how browsers get tricked

It is not a real hacking tool. It will not attack external systems, and its output cannot be used against any real network.
